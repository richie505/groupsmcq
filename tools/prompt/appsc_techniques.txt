The notes are the "Combined Notes" for APPSC Group 1 and Group 2 (History & Culture, Polity/Society/IR, Economy, Geography, Science-Tech-Environment, Current Affairs). Frame questions for both exams' objective papers.


STEP 4 — BUILD EVERY QUESTION THE WAY APPSC ACTUALLY BUILDS IT
The rules below come from "APPSC MCQ Techniques – Tested on PYQs", a study of 29,854 past questions (APPSC, UPSC and other state papers) that measured how setters write true and false statements and how answer keys are distributed. Questions must follow these measured patterns, so that practising them trains the same instincts the real paper rewards. Never mention the techniques inside a question.

A. Question-type mix (let the content decide, but aim near APPSC's real mix)
* About half: direct one-fact questions (who / which / where / when / what term). Almost half of real APPSC questions are name, place or one-word answers.
* About 1 in 5: statement questions with coded options in APPSC style — "1 and 2 only", "1, 2 and 3", "Only a, b and d", "All of the above". Number the statements 1., 2., 3. (or a., b., c.) on separate lines. Do NOT use "How many of the above are correct?" (a UPSC style APPSC has not used) except very rarely.
* About 1 in 10: Match List-I with List-II. Put List-I items as a., b., c., d. and List-II as i., ii., iii., iv. in the question, and give the options as codes like "a–ii, b–iv, c–i, d–iii".
* About 1 in 16: Assertion (A) – Reason (R).
* About 1 in 20: chronological / sequence ordering ("Arrange… in chronological order", or north-to-south etc.), items labelled A, B, C, D and options like "B, D, C, A".
* About 1 in 10: NOT / EXCEPT / "not correctly matched" / odd-one-out questions. Write NOT and EXCEPT in capitals in the question.

B. How to write a FALSE statement or a wrong option (the traps APPSC uses — each measured to point to WRONG statements)
* Extreme words: "only", "all the", "any", "entirely", "exclusively", "cannot", "never", "always".
* A "not / does not" that flips a true fact.
* Wrong person with a deed: "during the reign of…", "founded by…", "started by…", "built by…", "written by…" with a swapped ruler/founder/author (the strongest APPSC trap: 75 in 100 such statements were wrong).
* Wrong authority holding a power: "The President / Governor / Speaker / Chief Minister can / may / appoints / removes / dissolves…" given to the wrong authority. In Polity, "can / may" statements about powers are usually the FALSE ones.
* Wrong body: "launched / developed / set up by…", "Ministry of… / Department of…" swapped with a neighbouring ministry; a world body (UN, UNEP, WHO, World Bank, IMF, WEF, ILO…) swapped with another.
* Wrong membership or composition: "member of…", "consists of…", "comprises…".
* Wrong Article / provision: "Article N…", "according to the Constitution…".
* Wrong range, trend or share: "between X and Y", "in the last decade", "steadily", a changed percentage.
* Reversed comparison: "higher than", "larger than", "whereas", "unlike", "but".
* Wrong location: "is located in", "lies in", "headquartered at".
* Wrong size claim: "largest in India", "in the world", "in the state".
* True start, false end: a long sentence (25+ words) whose last detail is wrong.
* Swapped meanings of paired terms (GDP/GNP, repo/reverse repo, endemic/exotic, Union/State List, statutory/constitutional).
* Look-alike word: an everyday word in place of the exact official word (the Preamble says "Liberty", not "Freedom").
* Famous-word trap: a well-known scheme, mission or event with the wrong year, ministry, figure or partner.
Keep the traps a tendency, not a giveaway: roughly 1 in 4 statements that carry "only / all / never" should still be TRUE (when the notes support it), and not every false statement should carry a clue word.
TRUE statements in real papers usually read as: what something does ("is used for", "helps in", "leads to", "aims to"), careful words ("some", "one of the"), and "can" in Science and Geography.

C. Answer-key balance (match APPSC so the tested tricks work on these questions exactly as on the real paper)
* Statement questions: about 65 in 100 individual statements TRUE. When "All of the above" / "1, 2 and 3" is offered it should be the key in about 30–40% of those questions.
* A statement that appears in 3 or 4 of the four coded options should be TRUE about 80% of the time; build the distractor codes around the key the way real papers do.
* Assertion–Reason: always these four options, in this order:
  a: Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  b: Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  c: A is true but R is false
  d: A is false but R is true
  Key distribution across your A–R questions: about 45% (a), 35% (b), 20% (c) or (d).
* "All of the above" as an option: key about 40% of the time. "None of the above": key about 30% of the time (it is a real option in APPSC, never filler). Offer either in only about 1 of every 8 questions.
* Number or year options: four distinct values in ascending order; the key is one of the two middle values about 60% of the time.
* Sentence options: the correct one is the most precise (often slightly longer) about a third of the time — not always.
* Match the following: wrong codes should share one or two correct pairs with the key (so "start from your sure pair" and "the pair repeated in most options" both work); no code may repeat a List-II item.
* Chronology: about half the wrong sequences share the correct first item; they differ mostly in the middle.
* Across all questions spread correct_option evenly over a, b, c, d.

STEP 5 — UNIQUE, NON-REDUNDANT QUESTIONS
* Each question must test a DIFFERENT fact or a genuinely different angle. Never write two questions whose correct answer is the same fact in different words.
* A question listed under "ALREADY IN THE BANK" (past exam questions and questions already written for this section) must not be repeated, rephrased or reversed. Test the other facts instead; if a fact is covered only by a bank question, move on.
* Distractors must come from nearby facts in the same notes (other rulers of the dynasty, other articles of the Part, other schemes of the ministry) — never random filler and never options that are obviously absurd.
* Each question must stand alone: no "the above notes", no "as mentioned", no reference to other questions.
* Facts only from the notes. If the notes are ambiguous or look wrong on a point, skip that point rather than guessing.
